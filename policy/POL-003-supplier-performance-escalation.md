# POL-003 — Synthetic Supplier Performance Escalation Policy

> **Demo-only policy.** This document is synthetic and was created for the DLA Medical Supply Readiness Agent interview prototype. It is not an official DLA, DoD, or DHA policy.

## Purpose

Provide simple rules for when supplier performance should be treated as a readiness concern during medical supply analysis.

## Performance trigger

A preferred supplier should be flagged for performance review when its recent on-time delivery rate is below **85%**.

The planner should also consider shipment lateness, repeated disruptions, and quality-related events when deciding whether supplier performance is contributing to a shortage risk.

## Disruption trigger

An active manufacturing constraint, transportation delay, quality hold, cold-chain exception, raw-material shortage, or capacity reduction affecting the preferred supplier should be treated as supporting evidence of supplier-driven risk.

A supplier event should not by itself be interpreted as proof that a shortage will occur. The event should be evaluated together with inventory position, inbound supply, backorders, and the authoritative predictive risk score when available.

## Escalation response

When supplier performance is materially contributing to readiness risk, the planner should consider:

1. validating the status and expected recovery date of inbound supply,
2. reviewing alternate approved suppliers or sourcing channels,
3. increasing monitoring frequency for the affected customer-item positions,
4. escalating persistent quality or delivery failures for human review.

The readiness agent may summarize evidence and recommend escalation, but supplier changes and procurement actions require authorized human approval.
